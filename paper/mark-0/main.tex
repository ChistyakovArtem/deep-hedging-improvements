\begin{abstract}
Deep hedging replaces a hand-designed hedge with a policy trained end to end
against a risk functional, but reported performance is sensitive to representation,
initialization, state design, and optimization.  We study a different question from
proposing another isolated architecture: can a small, reproducible sequence of
validated choices turn the standard pathwise deep-hedging policy into a strong
default?  Our protocol is inspired by recipe-oriented tabular learning.  It adds
one component family at a time---periodic numerical embeddings, residual prediction
around a model hedge, running profit and loss, modern optimizers, and stochastic
exploration---and freezes each choice using a development panel that is disjoint
from the final benchmark.  The selected policy is then compared with
information-matched analytical and numerical hedges on one-factor-at-a-time
slices of GBM, Heston, and rough-Bergomi markets, two payoffs, transaction
costs, moneyness, and rebalancing frequencies.  The main comparator under
Heston is the local minimum-variance delta, not merely the price delta.  We
specify paired multi-seed evaluation, dimensionless risk normalization,
tail-risk and turnover metrics, and a one-class software interface.
Preliminary single-execution Heston results motivate the study but are not
treated as confirmatory evidence; the paper's claims are intentionally
conditional on the locked multi-seed benchmark.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Deep Hedging trains a parametric trading policy directly against a portfolio
risk measure and can incorporate transaction costs, liquidity restrictions, and
other non-classical frictions \cite{buehler2019deep}.  This flexibility makes it
an attractive alternative to deriving a new control solution for every market
and contract.  It also creates a practical ambiguity: two implementations of
``Deep Hedging'' can differ in input scaling, state variables, output
parameterization, initialization, optimizer, and exploration, with each choice
materially changing convergence.

The usual response is to introduce one more learning algorithm.  We instead
treat the complete training stack as the object of study.  This follows the
recipe-oriented logic of RealMLP, where carefully validated defaults and broad
evaluation made a simple model competitive without per-dataset tuning
\cite{holzmueller2024better}.  In hedging, the analogous deliverable is a
policy that an end user can instantiate once, while market dynamics, payoff,
and risk preferences remain explicit configuration.

This framing raises a stronger baseline requirement.  The plain Heston price
delta, $\partial C/\partial S$, ignores the locally hedgeable component of
variance shocks when spot and variance are correlated.  A stock-only
minimum-variance (MV) hedge corrects this through variance sensitivity
\cite{hull2017optimal}.  Therefore, beating a Black--Scholes or price-delta
baseline is not sufficient evidence for a strong recipe.  The central empirical
question is whether the recipe matches or improves upon the best
information-matched mathematical hedge reliably, and where it fails, rather
than whether it wins in one favorable training run.

The intended contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item a guided, stagewise search protocol that attributes gains to
  representation, anchor, state, optimizer, and stochasticity while keeping the
  final benchmark locked;
  \item a fair baseline suite centered on the MV delta and cost-aware
  no-trade policies, with the same state information available to learned and
  mathematical hedges;
  \item a multi-market, multi-contract benchmark using paired seeds and
  reporting risk, tail loss, turnover, fees, and deviation from the MV hedge;
  and
  \item a reusable \texttt{DeepHedger} class whose selected recipe is exposed as
  a documented default rather than notebook-specific code.
\end{enumerate}

The current manuscript is a mark-0 draft.  It fixes the claims, experimental
questions, table structure, and evidence standard before the confirmatory runs.
Numerical values in Section~\ref{sec:preliminary} are preliminary diagnostics
with explicit provenance.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Deep and reinforcement-learning hedges.}
Buehler et al.\ formulate hedging with frictions as direct optimization of
convex risk measures over neural policies \cite{buehler2019deep}.  Subsequent
work studies continuous-control and distributional objectives
\cite{cao2021deep}, actor--critic formulations that cover multiple portfolios
or risk aversions \cite{murray2022continuous,buehler2024bellman}, and policies
trained on empirical intraday data \cite{mikkila2023empirical}.  These works
establish that learning can outperform conventional deltas in suitable
settings.  They do not remove the need for controlled recipe selection against
the strongest model-consistent hedge.

\paragraph{Structured policies and optimization.}
Residual learning around a Black--Scholes delta can reduce the complexity of
the learned function \cite{qiao2024enhancing}.  K-FAC approximates neural
curvature with tractable Kronecker factors \cite{martens2015kfac}; recent work
applies related second-order structure to deep hedging and reports faster
optimization on a difficult cliquet problem \cite{mueller2024fast}.  The
current study asks whether residual anchors and curvature-aware optimization
remain useful after representation and state design are controlled.

\paragraph{Recipe transfer from tabular learning.}
Numerical feature embeddings with periodic activations can improve simple MLPs
\cite{gorishniy2022embeddings}; Fourier features provide a related mechanism
for representing high-frequency low-dimensional functions
\cite{tancik2020fourier}.  More recently, a benchmark of fifteen optimizers on
seventeen tabular datasets identified Muon, Schedule-Free AdamW, and AdamW with
an exponential moving average as strong candidates
\cite{gorishniy2026optimizers}; Schedule-Free learning itself removes the need
to prescribe the stopping horizon in advance \cite{defazio2024schedulefree}.
These results motivate candidates, but do not imply that their ranking transfers
to a path-dependent entropic hedging objective.  That transfer is an empirical
question.

\section{Hedging Problem}

We work on a discrete grid $0=t_0<\dots<t_N=T$.  Let
$B_t=\exp(rt)$, $\bar S_t=B_t^{-1}S_t$, and
$\bar H=B_T^{-1}H(S_T)$.  Before the move from $t_i$ to $t_{i+1}$, a policy
chooses $\delta_i$ shares.  In discounted units, with proportional cost $c$
and $\delta_{-1}=0$, terminal hedging profit and loss is
\begin{equation}
 \bar Z^\delta =
 \sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\delta_i(\bar S_{t_{i+1}}-\bar S_{t_i})
 -c\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\bar S_{t_i}\lvert\delta_i-\delta_{i-1}\rvert
 -\bar H.
\label{eq:pnl}
\end{equation}
An initial option premium would shift every strategy by the same constant and
does not affect their ranking under CARA/entropic risk, although it must be
restored for economically interpretable wealth levels.  Each contract declares
an ex-ante currency scale $s_H$: underlying notional for a vanilla call and
cash payout for a digital.  We set $\widetilde Z=\bar Z/s_H$ and use
dimensionless risk aversion $\alpha$.  This rule is fixed before simulation,
never estimated from test PnL, and prevents raw digital and vanilla notionals
from silently changing risk preferences.  The training objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}_\alpha(\delta)
 = \softmin_\alpha(\widetilde Z^\delta)
 = \frac{1}{\alpha}\log
   \mathbb{E}\!\left[\exp(-\alpha\widetilde Z^\delta)\right],
 \qquad \alpha>0,
\label{eq:softmin}
\end{equation}
where lower is better.  We do not describe this quantity as CVaR: final
evaluation reports lower-tail $\cvar_{95}$ and $\cvar_{99}$ separately.

\paragraph{CARA and running PnL.}
Entropic utility has constant absolute risk aversion: adding current wealth or
a fixed premium translates the certainty equivalent but does not change the
optimal action.  Running PnL is therefore not justified as wealth-dependent
risk preference.  It can help only as a proxy for omitted path information,
finite-sample optimization, or a state variable introduced by the precise
accounting/control formulation.  We triangulate the mechanism using
independent retraining with the feature on/off, inference-time masking, and a
fixed-state diagnostic that holds $(t_i,S_i,q_i,\delta_{i-1})$ constant while
sweeping $\widetilde G_i$ over empirical quantiles.  A reproducible seeded gain
may identify omitted history or an optimization scaffold, in which case the
feature is retained and its mechanism characterized.  Wealth-dependent
behavior, however, requires explicit constraints or a non-CARA objective.

\subsection{Information-matched mathematical hedges}

In undiscounted units, the Heston dynamics are
\begin{align}
 dS_t &= rS_t\,dt + S_t\sqrt{v_t}\,dW_t^S,\\
 dv_t &= \kappa(\theta-v_t)\,dt
       + \xi\sqrt{v_t}\,dW_t^v,\qquad
 d\langle W^S,W^v\rangle_t=\rho\,dt,
\end{align}
let $C$ denote the option price as a function of time, spot, and variance.
We compute it from the characteristic-function representation
\cite{heston1993closed}.  Minimizing the instantaneous
conditional variance of $dC-\delta\,dS$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \delta_{\mathrm{MV}}
 = C_S + \frac{\rho\xi}{S}C_v .
 \label{eq:mvdelta}
\end{equation}
The implementation evaluates $C$, $C_S$, and $C_v$ in batched Torch code using
fixed Gauss--Legendre quadrature and automatic differentiation.  Crucially, the
network observes $v_t$ whenever the baseline uses it.  The comparison therefore
does not give the learned policy an unobserved-volatility advantage.

Transaction costs can make continuous retargeting to
Equation~\eqref{eq:mvdelta} suboptimal.  We consequently include a
validation-tuned no-trade band around the MV delta.  ``Best mathematical
baseline'' means the predeclared, validation-selected member of this baseline
family; it never means choosing the best test-set row after evaluation.  Under
rough Bergomi, Keller-Ressel derives analytic expressions for the
variance-optimal strategy and its mean-square error
\cite{bayer2016rough,kellerressel2022bartlett}.  We include rough Bergomi in
the headline benchmark only after the simulator's filtration and state
representation are matched to that result and our implementation agrees with
both the analytic expression and a converged conditional-covariance
calculation.  Otherwise those rows are deferred rather than compared against a
weak proxy.

\section{\methodname}

\subsection{One policy class, explicit configuration}

At time $t_i$, the deterministic policy consumes
\begin{equation}
 x_i =
 [\,\log(S_{t_i}/K),\ q_i,\ \tau_i/T,\ \delta_{i-1},\
 \mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{PnL}}\widetilde{G}_i\,],
 \label{eq:features}
\end{equation}
where $q_i$ contains market state variables (for example $v_i$), $\tau_i$ is
time to maturity, and $\widetilde{G}_i$ is normalized running portfolio PnL.
Log-moneyness replaces raw spot to make scale transfer an explicit inductive
bias.  The public class exposes each term in Equation~\eqref{eq:features}, the
architecture, anchor, optimizer, and stochastic trainer as hyperparameters;
the paper ultimately selects one default configuration.

Two periodic representations are compared with a plain MLP.  A matrix-mixed
embedding uses
\[
 e_{\mathrm{mix}}(x)=[\sin(xB),\cos(xB)],
\]
while the feature-wise form assigns frequencies independently,
\[
 e_{\mathrm{fw}}(x_j)=
 [\sin(2\pi x_j c_j),\cos(2\pi x_j c_j)].
\]
Both frequency parameters are trainable.  Initial scale $\sigma$, number of
frequencies, and the inclusion of the linear projection are tuned inside the
representation stage, rather than fixed from a different application domain.

The output can be direct or residual:
\begin{equation}
 \delta_i = \delta_{\mathrm{anchor}}(t_i,S_{t_i},q_i)
            + f_\theta(x_i).
 \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
The candidate anchors are a local-GBM delta and the strongest
information-matched model hedge.  Zero initialization of the last layer makes
the initial policy exactly equal to the anchor.  This separates learning a
correction from rediscovering the full hedge.

Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} replaces a full Cartesian grid with an auditable
sequence of conditional questions.  Each stage uses common random numbers
across candidates and aggregates ranks across a fixed development panel.  A
candidate is promoted only if its paired mean improvement is positive and its
uncertainty is acceptable; otherwise the incumbent is retained.  The selection
log records all candidates, so negative transfer remains visible.

Greedy search can miss interactions.  We therefore reserve a small
interaction audit after Stage~5: remove each selected component once, and test
the two most plausible pairwise interactions (embedding--anchor and
anchor--PnL).  This retains the interpretability and compute advantages of
guided search without pretending that component effects are additive.

\paragraph{Optimizer stage.}
The candidate set is Adam, AdamW, K-FAC, Schedule-Free AdamW, AdamW with
weight EMA, and Muon \cite{jordan2024muon}.  The 2026 tabular benchmark is a
motivation for including the last three, not evidence that they will win in
hedging.  Every optimizer
receives a comparable learning-rate and regularization budget; wall-clock time
and simulator paths are reported alongside epochs.

\paragraph{Stochastic stage.}
The current prototype adds Gaussian action noise and an annealed entropy bonus
to the pathwise objective.  This is a maximum-entropy pathwise policy, not full
Soft Actor-Critic: SAC is specifically an off-policy actor--critic algorithm
with a learned critic \cite{haarnoja2018sac}.  The final paper will use the label
\emph{SAC} only for an implementation with critic, targets, and replay.  The
stage tunes initial action standard deviation, entropy strength, and annealing;
it compares the deterministic incumbent, the pathwise MaxEnt policy, and full
SAC if the latter passes independent smoke tests.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Development and locked benchmark}

Recipe construction uses a predeclared, stratified Heston development panel
covering low/high vol-of-vol, zero/nonzero cost, in/out-of-the-money calls, and
short/long rebalancing grids.  The panel is fixed before Stage~1.  Five fully
seeded runs per candidate are used during selection.  Torch CPU/CUDA, NumPy,
initialization, simulator streams, and stochastic actions all receive explicit
seeds.

The frozen recipe is then evaluated with ten fresh seeds on the isolated
benchmark slices below.  This is deliberately not the Cartesian product of all
levels.  Within each market, the reference cell is an at-the-money call with
10-bp costs, $N=60$, and $\alpha=1$; one axis changes at a time while every
other coordinate stays at its reference.  The exact slices and a small,
predeclared cross-factor stress set are frozen before final evaluation.

The market slice follows complete $\rightarrow$ Markov stochastic volatility
$\rightarrow$ non-Markov rough volatility.  GBM with zero costs is a mandatory
sanity check: with finer rebalancing, the
learned hedge should approach the Black--Scholes hedge
\cite{black1973pricing}.  Heston is the main controlled incomplete market.
Rough Bergomi tests whether a recipe selected in a Markov setting transfers to
history-dependent volatility.  A Bates jump-diffusion
\cite{bates1996jumps} is a useful optional stress test, but will not displace
the three primary market families unless compute and page budget permit.

\subsection{Baselines, metrics, and statistical reporting}

Every applicable slice cell contains: no hedge; model price delta; local MV delta;
validation-tuned MV no-trade band; a converged numerical conditional-MV hedge
when no analytic counterpart exists; standard Deep Hedging with the base MLP;
and the frozen \methodname.  All policies use the same simulated state
information and trading dates.

The primary endpoint is test $\softmin_\alpha$ of dimensionless PnL.
Secondary endpoints are normalized PnL mean
and standard deviation, lower-tail $\cvar_{95}$ and $\cvar_{99}$, mean fees,
turnover $\sum_i|\delta_i-\delta_{i-1}|$, training wall time, and
\begin{equation}
 D_{\mathrm{MV}} =
 \frac{\mathbb{E}\sum_i S_{t_i}
       (\delta_i-\delta_{\mathrm{MV},i})^2}
      {\mathbb{E}\sum_i S_{t_i}},
\end{equation}
which measures economically weighted deviation from the local MV hedge.
Results are mean $\pm$ standard deviation over seeds.  The main comparison also
reports the paired seed-level gap and a 95\% bootstrap confidence interval.
Tables retain every prespecified benchmark cell; aggregate ranks cannot hide a
failed market or payoff.

\section{Reproducibility and Software Contract}

Every run will be generated from a versioned configuration containing market,
payoff, method, and seed.  A per-run entry point writes a machine-readable
report, status, and completion marker; aggregation reads only reports and
produces paper tables without notebook copy-and-paste.  Repeating a completed
configuration is idempotent.  The release will include:

\begin{itemize}
  \item deterministic seed handling for NumPy, Torch, CUDA, simulator streams,
  weight initialization, and stochastic actions;
  \item unit tests comparing batched Heston price/MV deltas with the retained
  SciPy reference on a grid of spot, variance, and maturity;
  \item an accounting equivalence test showing that discounted-path PnL and an
  undiscounted self-financing cash account produce the same result;
  \item configuration manifests for the development ladder and locked
  benchmark, plus the exact aggregation script; and
  \item a pip-installable \texttt{DeepHedger} whose constructor accepts the
  same explicit hyperparameters used by the experiments and whose
  \texttt{recipe="default"} resolves to the frozen selection.
\end{itemize}

The default should be a named, immutable recipe version.  Adding every
experimental branch to one class is valid only if configuration validation,
serialization, and backward compatibility are tested; otherwise a nominally
simple API merely hides irreproducible notebook state.

\section{Limitations and Scope}

The study trains a policy for a specified simulated market and contract.  It
does not claim a single amortized hedge for arbitrary portfolios, robustness to
market misspecification, or real-data superiority.  Those are separate
questions.  The greedy search is compute-efficient but can miss higher-order
component interactions; the interaction audit only partially addresses this.
Analytical and nested-Monte-Carlo baselines also encode model knowledge that
may not be available in deployment, but this is deliberate: a method should
first be judged against the strongest fair oracle in simulation before
weaker-information comparisons are used.

Simulation access can make end-to-end pathwise gradients more sample-efficient
than model-free RL, so SAC is not expected to win by default.  A negative SAC
result remains informative if implementation and tuning are credible.  Finally,
recent empirical work shows that learned delta corrections can be regime
fragile \cite{zernikov2026what}; interpretability metrics and broad simulated
coverage reduce, but do not eliminate, that deployment risk.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper is designed around a claim that can fail cleanly.  A useful
\methodname{} must survive seeded selection, match or improve aggregate
performance against a strong information-matched hedge on a locked and
heterogeneous benchmark, characterize the cells in which it fails, and expose
the chosen recipe as reproducible software.  It need not win every cell.  The
current evidence is sufficient to motivate the guided search, but not to
declare a winner.
